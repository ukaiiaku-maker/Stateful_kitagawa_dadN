# Shared-root Peak stress-scale audit

Commit `f0b5a71` remains the coupled shared-root qualification milestone.  This
audit uses only completed Peak, 300 K checkpoints.  It launches no PD trajectory
and makes no production-physics change.

Machine-readable results are generated by
`scripts/audit_v9_shared_root_stress_scale.py`.  FEM phase tensors are
deterministically reconstructed from accepted `ep_gp`, `u`, mesh, and loading
arrays; signed-MPZ state is restored from the immutable shared-root capsule.
Reported checkpoint action is direct.  Alternative `m` values, stationary-cycle
quantiles, ordered-phase comparisons, flaw estimates, and multiplicity shifts
are read-only counterfactual calculations.

## Geometry transfer

For an elliptical notch, the Inglis estimate is
`Kt = 1 + 2 a/b = 1 + 2 sqrt(a/rho)`.

| geometry | a (um) | b (um) | rho nominal (um) | analytical Kt | observed FEM Kt |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| historical K360 | 360 | 127.279 | 45 | 6.657 | 5.323 at the 735.921 MPa failure anchor; 4.273 in the evolved 690.443 MPa control |
| blunt production target | 150 | 300 | 600 | 2.000 | 2.034 |

The elastic FEM concentration ratio is `5.323/2.034 = 2.62`.  Holding the same
root stress therefore requires about 2.62 times more remote stress solely from
the geometry transfer.  The remote maxima are 1.635 GPa for the historical
735.921 MPa-amplitude K360 anchor and 5.556 GPa at blunt-notch 2500 MPa, a
factor 3.40.  Geometry explains most, but not all, of that increase.

## Provenance and endpoint semantics of m=3

The authoritative `sharp_front.py` sets `m_hits=3`, `tau_c=1 us` and describes
it verbatim in code as **“cooperative bonds per advance increment.”**  The
configuration comments likewise say the completed event advances one front
increment through cooperative kink-pair/bond-rupture events over a front
correlation length.  The v10.2.30 energy gate then assigns resistance using
`m_hits * DeltaG_cleave` and proposes a crack-event length.

Therefore the m=3 renewal is not an elementary cleavage-attempt clock.  It is
a completed sharp-front rupture/advance process.  The PD shared-root branch
currently uses that completed-event clock to inject a reversible embryo and
then independently applies healing/stabilization and spatial front topology.
That is an endpoint-semantic overlap and likely double-counts completion or
stable-birth filtering.  It is not yet proven that the PD stabilization clock
represents a distinct physical process absent from sharp-front completion.

Recommendation: keep current m=3 results as a strict parity control.  For a PD
production model whose global event is an elementary cleavage attempt, the
physics-consistent candidate baseline is m=1 followed by the existing PD
embryo transition and topology.  Do not enact this change until that endpoint
choice is explicitly accepted.

## Physical weakest-link multiplicity

The global action must remain invariant to PD candidate count.  The audited
row supplies a 10 um reference front width and 25 um2 source area; its aggregate
emission multiplicity (about 352,563 sources per slip system) belongs to MPZ
plasticity, not cleavage weakest-link replication.

One global cleavage clock is therefore most defensibly one correlated 10 um
front segment / 25 um2 process area.  A specimen-level multiplicity would be
`M = W_physical/10 um` (or physical process area divided by 25 um2).  The 2-D
model has no specified out-of-plane specimen width, so numerical PD points,
the 3804 candidate sites, or the ellipse perimeter cannot supply `M`.

For a stationary clock, physical multiplicity divides every attempt quantile
by `M`.  Around 2000--2500 MPa the computed m=3 slope is about 38--45 MPa per
decade of action.  Consequently `M=10,100,1000` would shift an equal-life stress
downward by only about 40, 80, and 120 MPa, respectively.  This is material but
cannot explain a 1--2 GPa discrepancy and must not be inserted without specimen
front-width provenance.

The continuum quantities are phase-integrated, per-Gauss-point scalar-chain
counts.  Emission is the controlling per-source step; large downstream Taylor
availability cannot create flow without emission.  The signed MPZ separately
uses the audited aggregate persistent-emission multiplicity.  Its per-cycle
emitted increment is only order `1e-17`, shielding is numerically negligible,
and the largest stored backstress is only 1.30 MPa.

There is no feedback from signed-MPZ slip, blunting, shielding, or backstress
into the continuum FEM equilibrium.  Thus 9--11 GPa local elastic stresses do
not relax through MPZ plasticity.  `sigma/E < 0.05` establishes only a
small-strain kinematic range; it is not a strength, yield, or constitutive
validity test.  The negligible relaxation is a model-coupling diagnosis, not
evidence that tungsten remains elastic at those stresses.

## Two-scale notch diagnosis

To map 2500 MPa amplitude to the historical experimental-scale bracket of
roughly 690--840 MPa while retaining the same root drive requires an additional
microstructural amplification of `2500/840=2.98` to `2500/690=3.62` beyond the
600 um macronotch field.  In an Inglis micro-ellipse this corresponds to
`a_micro/rho_micro=((Kt_micro-1)/2)^2 = 0.98--1.72`; for a 1 um root radius the
associated flaw depth is about 1.0--1.7 um.

Equivalently, under the current K-to-stress radius mapping, amplification
`sqrt(600 um/r_micro)=2.98--3.62` requires an effective microstructural radius
of about 68--46 um.  These are diagnoses, not tuning factors.  A production
two-scale model needs independently measured flaw/process-zone dimensions and
must avoid applying both Inglis and K-radius amplification to the same defect.

## Phase ordering

One-cycle phase-averaged versus ordered phase-resolved signed-MPZ replays give
m=3 action differences from `2e-14` relative (1000 MPa) to `5.5e-11` relative
(2150 MPa), and below `3e-15` at 2500 MPa.  Backstress differences are tiny.
Emission increments differ by factors about 1.1--2 because the absolute
increments are only `1e-18--1e-17`; they do not produce meaningful shielding.
The current stress-scale discrepancy is therefore not caused by phase-average
hazard quadrature on these stored cycles.  Ordered evolution remains the safer
production convention for future nonstationary/high-emission states.

## Factor decomposition and baseline recommendation

1. Amplitude-to-maximum convention: factor `2/(1-R)=2.222`; 2500 MPa becomes
   5.556 GPa remotely.
2. Blunt-notch FEM Kt: factor 2.034; K360 was about 5.323 at its failure anchor.
3. Cleavage critical-stress surface: Peak has approximately 7.57 GPa critical
   stress at 300 K, placing the 7.5--9.35 GPa exact-root range on a steep rate
   flank.
4. m-hit renewal: dominant suppressor; at 2500 MPa it changes median attempt
   life from 1.23 cycles (m=1) to 3.70e5 cycles (m=3).
5. MPZ shielding: negligible in these checkpoints.
6. Physical correlation multiplicity: presently one 10 um correlated segment;
   unknown specimen width prevents a specimen-level multiplier. Plausible
   decade-scale multiplicities shift stress by tens, not thousands, of MPa.
7. Plastic relaxation: continuum emission is vanishingly small and MPZ state
   does not relax FEM stress, leaving a nearly elastic 9--11 GPa notch field.

No new stress should be selected from the present m=3 ladder.  The recommended
corrected baseline is: retain the measured macronotch elastic FEM field; define
an independently justified microflaw/process-zone scale; use one clock per
physical correlation segment; and resolve whether the global event is an
elementary m=1 attempt or a completed m=3 front increment before coupling it to
PD healing/stabilization.  Until that endpoint decision and the missing
MPZ-to-FEM relaxation choice are made, the absolute physical stress scale is
**undetermined**, although the existing runs remain valid parity controls.
